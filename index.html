How It Works

Light Source Model

All scenarios start with a light whose output is specified as
illuminance at a reference distance:

  E₀  =  illuminance (fc or lux) at d₀

The inverse square law then gives illuminance at any other distance d:

  E  =  E₀ × (d₀ / d)²

This is exact for a point source and a very good approximation
for most production fixtures.

Point Light

Direct calculation:

  E_subject  =  E₀ × (d₀ / d)²
  Beam diameter  =  2d × tan(θ / 2)

No intermediate surface — light goes straight to the subject.

Bounce Light

A matte surface is illuminated and re-emits light toward the subject.

  E₁  =  E₀ × (d₀ / d₁)²          illuminance at surface
  L   =  ρ × E₁ / π                 Lambertian luminance
  E₂  =  L × Ω                      illuminance at subject

Ω is the solid angle of the effective source area as seen from
the subject. If the beam footprint is smaller than the material,
only the illuminated area contributes — the calculator
automatically uses a circle solid angle in that case.

Diffusion

Identical to bounce but with a material-specific forward bias:

  L  =  τ × E₁ × bias / π

Diffusion materials scatter more light forward than a perfect
Lambertian surface, modelled here as a multiplier on luminance.
Material-specific bias values range from ~1.05× (Light Opal —
mild forward preference) to ~1.15× (Full Grid Cloth).

Booklight

A three-stage cascade — light hits a bounce card,
reflects to a diffusion panel, then transmits to the subject:

  Stage 1 — Light → Bounce
    E₁  =  E₀ × (d₀ / d₁)²

  Stage 2 — Bounce → Diffusion
    L₁  =  ρ × E₁ / π
    E₂  =  L₁ × Ω₁          (bounce solid angle to diffusion)

  Stage 3 — Diffusion → Subject
    L₂  =  τ × E₂ × bias / π
    E₃  =  L₂ × Ω₂          (diffusion solid angle to subject)

f-stop Formula

The app uses the incident-light exposure equation:

  N²  =  E_fc × ISO × t / C    (C = 25 for foot-candles)
       =  E_lux × ISO × t / 250  (equivalent in lux)

This matches a Sekonic incident meter reading. The constant
C = 25 is the foot-candle equivalent of the standard lux
incident constant (~250).

Note: C = 12.5 would be correct only if E were luminance
in cd/m² (reflected-light formula) — an earlier version of
this calculator used that constant incorrectly with illuminance,
producing results about 4.5 stops too high.

Solid Angle

Earlier versions used the paraxial approximation Ω ≈ A/d²,
which accumulates error when surfaces are close to the subject.

This version uses exact formulas:

  Rectangle (half-dims a, b at distance d):
    Ω  =  4 · arctan( a·b / (d·√(a²+b²+d²)) )

  Circle (radius r at distance d):
    Ω  =  2π · (1 − d/√(d²+r²))

The calculator automatically selects the circle formula when
the beam footprint is smaller than the material, and the
rectangle formula when the beam covers the full material.

Assumptions

• Surfaces are perfectly matte (Lambertian) — specular
  or semi-specular materials are not supported
• Light is centred on each surface and aimed perpendicularly
• Beam is circular with uniform illumination across
  its footprint — real beams are brighter at the center
  and fall off toward the edges
• Each surface has uniform luminance — the calculator
  computes brightness at the center of the material and
  applies it to the entire area
• In booklight mode, non-uniform illumination compounds
  across both stages (bounce → diffusion → subject)

Accuracy

The solid angle formulas are exact for the geometry —
they correctly account for light arriving from steep
angles at the edges of large, close sources.

The main source of error is the uniform luminance
assumption. When the beam barely covers the material,
the center is significantly brighter than the edges,
but the calculator treats them equally.

This matters most when:
• The beam diameter is close to the material size
• The subject is very close to a large source
• Multiple stages cascade (booklight)

For typical studio setups, expect results within
±1/3 to ±1/2 stop of a meter reading — well within
working tolerance for pre-lighting estimates. Always
meter on set for critical exposure.

Materials Reference

Important Note

This calculator models only perfectly diffuse (Lambertian) surfaces.
Specular or semi-specular materials — silver laminates, mirrors,
metallic reflectors — are NOT supported and will produce
inaccurate results.

Bounce Materials (reflectivity ρ)

Ultrabounce          0.85
White Foam Core      0.85
Bleached Muslin      0.80
Unbleached Muslin    0.65
Beadboard            0.85

Values are approximate — manufacturer and age affect results.

Full Silk            0.38  /  1.12×

Quarter Silk         0.70  /  1.08×
Light Opal           0.75  /  1.05×

Forward bias represents how much light is concentrated forward
relative to a perfect Lambertian surface (bias = 1.0).
Higher bias = slightly broader scatter; lower bias = more directional.

Values are from manufacturer data sheets and field measurements. They may vary by product condition and manufacturer.
